Overview: Three-Target ST-IAT Design

This protocol describes three separate Single-Target Implicit Association Tests (ST-IATs), with respondents randomly assigned to one. This design keeps individual burden manageable while allowing measurement of implicit attitudes toward three theoretically distinct targets relevant to the study of rebel territorial control and national identity formation.

Target What It Measures Theoretical Link
Other Congolese Inter-regional openness, national integration Contact/displacement mechanism; national identity breadth
Congolese State Attitude toward state institutions Institutional substitution mechanism
Outsiders (Rwanda/Uganda) In-group boundary definition Ethnic mobilization; who counts as "us" vs. "them"

Each respondent completes one ST-IAT (~5-7 minutes), assigned randomly. With a target sample of 2,400-3,000, this yields approximately 800-1,000 respondents per target—sufficient statistical power for detecting meaningful effects.

📊 Methodological Reference

This design follows Lowes & Montero (2021, QJE) who used a Single-Target IAT (ST-IAT) to measure implicit attitudes toward local chiefs in eastern DRC. Their approach used audio stimuli and demonstrated feasibility in low-literacy, conflict-affected contexts.

Design Principles

1. Audio-Based Stimuli

All stimuli are audio recordings of words/phrases. This addresses:

  • Illiteracy (common in rural North Kivu)
  • Varying reading speeds that contaminate reaction times
  • Cross-linguistic accessibility

2. Swahili Primary Language

Record stimuli in Swahili with French backup:

  • Widely understood across ethnic groups in eastern DRC
  • Not associated with any single ethnic group (unlike Lingala)
  • Language of daily commerce and interaction

3. Neutral Target Stimuli

Target stimuli must be evaluatively neutral—they should evoke the category without being inherently positive or negative. This is essential for valid measurement.

4. Sensitivity Protocol

For the Rwanda/Uganda target especially:

  • Enumerator safety assessment before administration
  • Option to skip if security context is dangerous
  • Careful framing that doesn't prime hostility

5. Tablet + Headphones

Administer on tablets with headphones to:

  • Ensure audio clarity
  • Maintain privacy (others can't hear)
  • Reduce social desirability effects

Shared Evaluative Stimuli

The Good/Bad words are identical across all three IATs. This allows comparability and reduces development burden.

Selection Criteria

  • Clearly positive or negative
  • Not associated with national/ethnic/regional identity
  • Not associated with conflict, state, or armed groups
  • Common words known to low-education respondents
  • Similar length and frequency across Good/Bad

⚠️ Note on "Amani" (Peace)

"Peace" is potentially problematic because it's central to political discourse in DRC and "peace agreements" are associated with specific political processes. Decision: Keep it, but monitor in pilot. If error rates are high, replace with "Afya" (Health) or "Tumaini" (Hope).

ST-IAT 1: Other Congolese

Conceptual Target

"Congolese people from other provinces"—specifically, people from the west/Kinshasa region, which is the relevant "other" for North Kivu respondents.

Target Stimuli: Other Congolese

Kinois
Person from Kinshasa
Mtu wa Kinshasa
Person from Kinshasa (Swahili)
Katangais
Person from Katanga
Mtu wa Lubumbashi
Person from Lubumbashi
Mukongo
Person from Kongo Central
Lingala
The Lingala language

Why These Stimuli Work

  • All are recognizable to North Kivu respondents as "other Congolese"
  • None are evaluatively loaded—they're neutral descriptors of regional origin
  • They represent the relevant comparison group—western/central DRC
  • They're diverse—not just Kinshasa, but multiple regions

Potential Problems and Solutions

Problem Solution
"Katangais" has specific connotations (mining, Tshombe history) Monitor in pilot; replace with "Mtu wa Kasaï" if needed
"Lingala" is a language, not a person Pilot whether respondents categorize it correctly
Some respondents might be from these regions originally Add screener question; if from these regions, assign to different IAT

ST-IAT 2: The Congolese State

Conceptual Target

"The Congolese state/government"—the institutional apparatus, not specific politicians.

Target Stimuli: Congolese State

Serikali
Government
Drapeau ya Congo
Congolese flag
FARDC
Congolese armed forces
Polisi
Police
Carte d'identité
National ID card
Kinshasa
The capital (seat of government)

Why These Stimuli Work

  • Represent different aspects of state presence: military, administrative, symbolic
  • Recognizable even to low-education respondents
  • Mostly evaluatively neutral as concepts (attitudes toward them vary—that's what we measure)

Potential Problems and Solutions

Problem Solution
FARDC has very negative associations in North Kivu (predation, abuse) This is what we want to measure—but monitor for ceiling effects
"Kinshasa" might be categorized as "other Congolese" not "state" Pilot test categorization; if problematic, replace with "Bunge" (Parliament)
Current president might contaminate "government" Avoid any stimuli with specific politician names or images
"Carte d'identité" might evoke document access problems Pilot; could replace with "Hymne national" (national anthem)
ℹ️ Sensitivity Note

This IAT is moderately sensitive—negative attitudes toward the state could be seen as politically risky to express. The IAT's implicit nature partially addresses this. Use headphones so responses are private, and don't record which specific stimuli caused errors.

ST-IAT 3: Outsiders (Rwanda/Uganda)

Conceptual Target

"People from neighboring countries"—specifically Rwanda and Uganda, which are the relevant "outsiders" in the North Kivu context.

⛔ CRITICAL SENSITIVITY WARNING

This IAT measures implicit attitudes toward Rwandans and Ugandans. In North Kivu:

  • Rwanda is widely blamed for backing M23
  • "Rwandan" is sometimes used as an ethnic slur against Banyarwanda Congolese
  • Anti-Rwandan sentiment is politically mobilized
  • Expressing positive attitudes toward Rwanda could be dangerous for respondents

This IAT must be administered with extreme caution.

Safety Protocol for Outsider IAT

  1. Enumerator pre-assessment: Before administering, assess:
    • Is there active M23 presence within 10km? → Skip this IAT
    • Has there been recent anti-Rwandan violence in this area? → Skip
    • Does respondent appear anxious about this topic? → Skip
  2. Explicit framing: "This is about people from neighboring countries in general, not about any armed groups or politics"
  3. Voluntary opt-out: Respondent can skip at any time without explanation
  4. No identifiable data: Don't record which respondents had extreme scores

Target Stimuli: Outsiders

Mtu wa Kampala
Person from Kampala

Why These Specific Stimuli

  • Include both Rwanda and Uganda—not just Rwanda—to avoid the IAT being transparently about M23/Rwanda politics
  • Use neutral geographic terms (person from Kigali, person from Kampala) alongside ethnic terms
  • "Jirani" (neighbor) is a softening term that captures cross-border relationship without ethnic specificity

Potential Problems and Solutions

Problem Solution
"Mnyarwanda" might refer to Congolese Banyarwanda, not Rwandans Add audio context: "Mnyarwanda kutoka Rwanda" (Rwandan from Rwanda)
Kinyarwanda is spoken by Congolese Tutsi/Hutu Pilot; if problematic, replace with "Kigali" (city name)
Extreme anti-Rwandan bias might produce ceiling effects This is informative—document the distribution
Security risk to enumerators Strict safety protocol; skip in high-risk areas
⚠️ Interpretation Caution

Results from this IAT must be interpreted carefully:

  • Strong negative implicit attitudes toward outsiders might reflect legitimate grievances about foreign interference
  • Or might reflect ethnic prejudice that extends to Congolese Banyarwanda
  • Cross-tabulate with explicit measures of Banyarwanda attitudes to disentangle

ST-IAT Block Structure

Following the standard ST-IAT protocol (same structure for all three IATs):

1
Practice Evaluative Sorting — 24 trials
← GOOD BAD →
2
Combined Block A — 24 trials (TEST BLOCK)
← TARGET + GOOD BAD →
3
Re-practice Evaluative Sorting — 24 trials
← GOOD BAD →
4
Combined Block B — 24 trials (TEST BLOCK)
← GOOD TARGET + BAD →

Total: 96 trials, approximately 5-7 minutes

Counterbalancing

Randomize whether Block 2 or Block 4 comes first:

  • Half of respondents: Target+Good first (Blocks 1→2→3→4)
  • Half of respondents: Target+Bad first (Blocks 1→4→3→2)

This controls for order effects and practice effects.

Trial Structure (Each Trial)

  1. Fixation cross appears (500ms)
  2. Audio stimulus plays (~500-800ms)
  3. Respondent presses Left or Right key
  4. Record: Reaction time (ms), Correct/Incorrect
  5. If incorrect: Brief error feedback (red X, 200ms)
  6. Inter-trial interval (250ms)
  7. Next trial

Administration Script

Introduction (Read by Enumerator)

"Now we will do a short activity on the tablet. This is not a test—there are no right or wrong answers. We are interested in how quickly people sort different words.

You will hear words through the headphones. Your job is to sort each word into a category as quickly as you can. Press the LEFT button for words that belong to the category on the left. Press the RIGHT button for words that belong to the category on the right.

Go as fast as you can, but try not to make too many mistakes. If you make a mistake, you will see a red X—just keep going.

Do you have any questions?"

For Outsider IAT, Add This Framing

"Some of the words you will hear are about people from neighboring countries like Rwanda and Uganda. Remember, this is about people in general, not about politics or armed groups. Just sort the words as quickly as you can."

Scoring Algorithm

Use the D-score algorithm (Greenwald et al., 2003):

Step 1: Data Cleaning

  • Delete trials with RT > 10,000ms
  • Delete participants with >10% of trials with RT < 300ms (random responding)
  • Delete participants with error rate > 30% (not following instructions)

Step 2: Calculate Mean RTs

  • Mean_TargetGood: Mean RT for correct trials in Block 2 (Target + Good)
  • Mean_TargetBad: Mean RT for correct trials in Block 4 (Target + Bad)

Step 3: Calculate Pooled SD

SD_pooled: Standard deviation of correct RTs across both Block 2 and Block 4

Step 4: Calculate D-Score

D = (Mean_TargetBad − Mean_TargetGood) / SD_pooled

Interpretation

D-Score Interpretation
D > 0 Positive implicit attitude toward target (faster when Target + Good)
D < 0 Negative implicit attitude toward target (faster when Target + Bad)
D ≈ 0 Neutral implicit attitude

Typical Effect Sizes

  • D = 0.15: Small effect
  • D = 0.35: Medium effect
  • D = 0.65: Large effect

For analysis, regress D-scores on rebel exposure, controlling for covariates.

Pilot Testing Protocol

Before full deployment, conduct rigorous piloting in three phases:

Phase 1: Stimulus Categorization (n=30)

Goal: Verify stimuli are correctly categorized

Method:

  • Play each stimulus
  • Ask: "Is this word about [CATEGORY A] or [CATEGORY B]?"
  • Record categorization

Criterion: Keep stimuli with >90% correct categorization

Phase 2: Full IAT (n=50)

Goal: Test complete IAT, identify problems

Analyze:

  1. Error rates by stimulus: Any stimulus with >25% errors → review/replace
  2. D-score distribution: Should show variance (not clustered at zero)
  3. Completion time: Should be 5-7 minutes
  4. Respondent feedback: Any confusion? Discomfort?

Phase 3: Test-Retest Reliability (n=30 subset)

Goal: Verify stability of measurement

Method: Re-administer IAT to same respondents 1-2 weeks later

Criterion: Test-retest correlation r > 0.5

Potential Problems and Mitigations

Technology Failures

Issue Mitigation
Tablet battery dies Carry backup tablets and power banks
Audio doesn't play Test headphones before each session
Touch screen unresponsive Use physical button response box if available
Software crashes Auto-save data every block; have restart protocol

Respondent Difficulties

Issue Mitigation
Doesn't understand task Extended practice block; enumerator can demonstrate
Presses randomly Monitor error rates; exclude if >30% errors
Gets frustrated Remind them there's no right/wrong; they can stop anytime
Hearing impairment Use French text version as backup
Motor difficulties (slow pressing) Analyze within-person RT patterns, not absolute RT

Conceptual Contamination

Issue Mitigation
"Kinshasa" categorized as both "state" and "other Congolese" Keep targets in separate IATs (not combined)
Respondent is from target region Screener question; assign to different IAT
Target stimuli are evaluatively loaded Pilot test for valence ratings

Security and Ethics

Issue Mitigation
Outsider IAT feels dangerous to respondent Clear opt-out option; skip in high-risk areas
Data could identify anti-government attitudes No names attached to IAT data; encrypt storage
Enumerator sees someone's strong anti-state bias Train enumerators on confidentiality; they don't see D-scores

Pre-Deployment Checklist

  • Audio stimuli recorded in Swahili
  • Audio stimuli recorded in French (backup)
  • Stimuli pilot-tested for categorization (>90% correct)
  • Full IAT pilot-tested (n=50)
  • D-score distribution shows variance
  • Error rates acceptable (<20% average)
  • Test-retest reliability acceptable (r>0.5)
  • Tablet software tested and debugged
  • Enumerators trained on administration
  • Safety protocol for Outsider IAT documented
  • IRB approval includes IAT module
  • Data storage and encryption set up

Integration with Survey

Placement in Survey

Place the IAT after Module 4 (Exposure) and before Module 6 (Pre-treatment proxies).

Rationale:

  • After exposure questions so you haven't primed identity/state topics yet
  • Before pre-treatment questions so it doesn't contaminate recall of parents' attitudes
  • Provides a "break" from verbal questions (novelty refreshes attention)

Randomization

At the start of the IAT module, the tablet randomly assigns:

  • IAT version: Other Congolese (33%) vs. State (33%) vs. Outsider (33%)
  • Block order: Target+Good first (50%) vs. Target+Bad first (50%)
  • Language: Based on respondent preference stated earlier
